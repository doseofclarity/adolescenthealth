Adolescent Health
(10‑24 years)

"Investing in adolescents builds the foundation for a healthy adult population."

Overview & Epidemiology

Definitions (WHO, CHN White Book): Adolescence = 10‑20 years; Youth = 15‑24 years; Young people = 10‑24 years. The period of transition from childhood to adulthood.

Philippine Demographics

  • Young people (10‑24) account for over 30% of total Philippine population (approx. 26 million).
  • Young people have developed physiologic resistance against common acute infections.
  • Mortality from all causes for this age group: 0.87 per 100,000.
  • By age 24, 45% of women are already mothers.
  • Women 15‑24 have the highest unmet need for family planning at 26%.

Age‑Specific Definitions

  • Adolescence: 10‑20 years
  • Youth: 15‑24 years
  • Young people: 10‑24 years (combined group used in policy)
  • Early adolescence: 10‑14 years
  • Late adolescence: 15‑19 years
  • Emerging adulthood: 20‑24 years

Health Risks & Behaviors

Behavioral Risks

  • By age 19, 12% of young people are already sexually active.
  • Smoking prevalence among adolescents: 21%.
  • Social drinking: 41% admit to drinking alcohol.
  • Random drug testing in high school students: positivity rates 3% to 10%.
  • Drug use rate higher among high school students than college students.

Leading Cause of Death

  • Accidents and injuries are the most common cause of death among young people (10‑24).
  • Rate: 30.68 per 100,000 persons aged 10‑24 (2000 data).
  • About 22.52% of all deaths due to accidents/injuries are adolescents and youth.

Unmet need for family planning among 15‑24 year olds: 26% (highest of any age group).

📌 Key point: While adolescents are physically resilient, risk‑taking behaviors (substance use, early sexual activity) create significant health challenges.

Strategic Thrusts (DOH 2005-2010)

  • Come up with models for adolescent‑friendly health services and adolescent‑friendly environments. A comprehensive adolescent/young people’s health care package needs to be designed and implemented in coordination with other government agencies.
  • Organize and build the capability of young people to promote healthy lifestyles, including sound reproductive health practices, accident prevention, and promotion of sound policies on work to improve their health and quality of life.
  • Continue fertility awareness activities among high school teachers and students to reduce the proportion of unwanted pregnancies and unmet need for family planning among young people.

🎯 Goal: Create a supportive environment where adolescents can access age‑appropriate, confidential, and respectful health services.

Essential Health Care Package for Adolescents & Youth

Core Services (6 items)

  • Management of illness (common acute and chronic conditions).
  • Counseling on substance abuse, sexuality, and reproductive tract infections (STIs).
  • Nutrition and diet counseling (eating disorders, obesity, undernutrition).
  • Mental health (depression, anxiety, stress management, suicide prevention).
  • Family planning and responsible sexual behavior (contraception, abstinence, prevention of teenage pregnancy).
  • Dental care (oral health, caries prevention).

Additional Important Components

  • Adolescent‑friendly clinic setup (confidentiality, non‑judgmental staff).
  • Life skills education and peer counseling.
  • STI/HIV prevention and testing.
  • Violence and injury prevention (accidents, bullying, domestic violence).
  • Physical activity promotion.
  • Substance abuse rehabilitation referral.

📌 The package should be evidence‑based, integrated with school health programs, and accessible in both RHUs and community settings.

Adolescent Reproductive Health

Under the Philippine Reproductive Health framework, Adolescent Reproductive Health (ARH) is one of the 10 priority elements. It focuses on fertility awareness, human sexuality, and counseling to promote responsible sexuality among adolescents.

Key ARH Interventions

  • Age‑appropriate sexuality education.
  • Access to contraceptive information and services (with parental consent as required).
  • Prevention and management of teenage pregnancy.
  • STI and HIV/AIDS education and testing.
  • Counseling on responsible sexual behavior and delay of sexual debut.

Priority Concerns (Philippine setting)

  • Early sexual initiation (by age 19, 12% active).
  • High unmet need for family planning among 15‑24 year olds (26%).
  • Rising HIV incidence among young key populations (MSM, transgender women).
  • Teenage pregnancy leading to school dropout and maternal risks.
  • Lack of adolescent‑friendly reproductive health services.

⚕️ ARH services must be confidential, non‑judgmental, and accessible to overcome barriers that adolescents face in seeking reproductive health care.

PHN Role & Nursing Care

  • Advocate for adolescent‑friendly health corners in RHUs (confidential, separate hours, trained staff).
  • Conduct school‑based health education on puberty, nutrition, substance abuse, and reproductive health.
  • Provide individual counseling on contraception, STI prevention, and mental health.
  • Identify at‑risk adolescents (substance use, depression, risky sexual behavior) and refer appropriately.
  • Collaborate with teachers, parents, and barangay youth leaders to create supportive environments.
  • Implement programs on accident prevention (road safety, drowning, falls).
  • Promote healthy lifestyle: physical activity, balanced diet, avoidance of smoking/alcohol.
  • Maintain confidentiality and a non‑judgmental attitude when dealing with adolescents.
  • Link adolescents to social services for teenage pregnancy, abuse, or mental health crises.

📌 Key principle: Adolescents are more likely to seek care when services are youth‑friendly, confidential, and offered without stigma.
